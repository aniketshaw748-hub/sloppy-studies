[warm, clear teacher, friendly and patient] Today's question. Why is L R parsing good and attractive? Also explain its demerits. [pause] Let's start from zero. An L R parser reads the input from the left, one token at a time, and puts each token on a stack. That move is called shift. Think of Lego. You clip small bricks onto a pile, and when the top bricks form a known shape, you fuse them into one bigger block. That move is called reduce. [pause] So the question wants two things. Why L R is attractive, and its demerits. [pause] Reason one. It handles almost every programming language. If you can write a context free grammar for it, L R can usually parse it. [pause] Reason two. It is the most general parser that never backtracks. It never guesses and goes back, and it is just as fast as the simple methods. [pause] Reason three. It is more powerful than L L parsing. Every grammar that L L can handle, L R can handle too, but not the other way round. [pause] Reason four. It catches errors as early as possible, on the very token where the input stops making sense. [pause] Now the demerits. One. Building the tables by hand is far too much work, so you need a parser generator like YACC. [pause] Two. The tables are huge, especially for canonical L R one. [pause] Three. Ambiguous grammars cause conflicts, and you must rewrite the grammar. [pause] So in your exam, write this. Four merits: nearly all languages, no backtracking, more powerful than L L, early errors. Three demerits: hard by hand, big tables, conflicts. [pause] To remember: powerful, but heavy. Five marks. Secured.
