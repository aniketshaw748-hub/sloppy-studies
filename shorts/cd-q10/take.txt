[warm, clear teacher, friendly and patient] Today's question. Explain viable prefixes. [pause] Let's start from zero. A bottom up parser reads a sentence from the left and keeps what it has read on a stack. Think of holding words in your hand until they form a phrase you recognise. [pause] When the top of the stack matches the right side of a rule, the parser swaps it for the left side. That matching piece is the handle. [pause] So the question wants two things. What a viable prefix is, and why it matters. [pause] Take a tiny grammar. E goes to E plus T, or T. And T goes to id. Parsing id plus id, we pass through the form T plus id. In it, the handle is the T at the front, because the next step turns T into E. [pause] Now the definition. A viable prefix is a prefix of a right sentential form that does not go past the right end of the handle. [pause] Take the prefixes of T plus id. Empty is viable. T is viable, it ends right at the handle. But T plus is NOT viable. It sticks out past the handle. [pause] Why does it matter? The stack of a shift reduce parser always holds a viable prefix. While it does, no error has happened. The moment the stack is not a viable prefix, the parser reports an error. And the automaton built from L R zero items recognises exactly the viable prefixes. [pause] So in your exam, write this. The definition. The example T plus id, with its handle. Why the stack is always a viable prefix. [pause] To remember: never past the handle. Five marks. Secured.
